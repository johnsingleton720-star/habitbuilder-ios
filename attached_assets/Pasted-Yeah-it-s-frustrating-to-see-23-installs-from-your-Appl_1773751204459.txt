Typical Conversion Benchmarks in This Space (2025–2026 Data)
Habit trackers fall under Health & Fitness/Productivity categories, which are strong for subscriptions but have variable funnels:
•  Install to paid subscription (direct or after free tier): Often 1–5% overall for freemium apps. Median ~2–4% in many reports; top performers hit 5–10%+ with strong onboarding. Hard paywall apps convert higher (up to 10–11% download-to-paid by day 35), but freemium like yours trades volume for lower friction.
•  Install to trial/start free tier engagement: Higher, but in your case, the free tier (1 habit, 3 sessions/week) is the “trial.” If users engage deeply, upgrade rates improve.
•  Free tier to paid (Pro/Premium): 2–8% is common for freemium; great is 8–12%+. Health/Fitness apps often see stronger long-term (e.g., top quartile ~68% trial-to-paid in some cohorts, but that’s after trial starts).
•  Health & Fitness specifics: These apps lead in revenue per install (median ~$0.44 after 14 days, up to $4+ in top decile) due to habit-forming potential, but initial drop-off is high—many users install, try once, and ghost.
With only 23 installs, you’re in a small sample—statistically, expecting 0–2 conversions is normal at low volume. If this persists at scale (e.g., 100+ installs/week), it signals a funnel issue.
Why Users Install But Don’t Subscribe/Upgrade
Common reasons in habit trackers (drawn from user feedback, reviews, and industry patterns):
1.  Expectation mismatch: People search for “habit tracker” expecting simple checklists/streaks (like Streaks, Habitica free tier). Your AI-powered coaching (interview → personalized plan, guided sessions) is more advanced—great for committed users, but overwhelming or “too much” for casual ones who just want basic tracking. They install, see the AI interview or limited free tier, and bail without upgrading.
2.  Free tier limitations feel too restrictive too soon: Only 1 habit forever + 3 sessions/week is generous compared to some (e.g., hard limits on habits), but if users want to track 3–5 habits right away (common for new resolutions), they hit the wall fast. Reviews in similar apps complain about “stark limitations” pushing upgrades aggressively.
3.  Onboarding friction or value not immediate: The AI interview is a strong differentiator (personalized plan in seconds), but if it feels like work or doesn’t deliver “wow” quickly, users drop. No formal trial for paid tiers means they must upgrade to see unlimited/Pro features—no low-risk taste of full value.
4.  Habit-building paradox: Many download because they’re struggling with discipline/motivation. They install during a motivational spike, but real habit formation takes weeks. If they miss a day or two, guilt from streaks/notifications kicks in, leading to uninstall rather than upgrade. Apps built for “disciplined” users get downloaded by those who aren’t yet—mismatch kills retention.
5.  Subscription fatigue/price hesitation: Even at $6 Pro, some see it as “another subscription.” If the app doesn’t prove ROI fast (e.g., “this helped me stick to X habit”), they won’t pay. No visible lifetime option or deep discounts might hurt.
6.  Technical/UX edges: Post-install bugs, slow loading, unclear upgrade prompts, or poor App Store page screenshots not matching experience. Also, Apple sign-in is seamless, but if payments feel clunky…
Implications & Edge Cases
•  Positive: Low CPT means scalable acquisition. If you fix conversion, ROAS could be excellent (e.g., one $6/mo subscriber pays back ~18 installs at current costs).
•  Risk: High churn potential if upgrades happen but users cancel after 1–2 months (common in habits—~half drop after 60 days).
•  Edge cases:
	•  Niche targeting (e.g., “AI habit coach” keywords) might attract better-fit users who convert higher.
	•  Seasonal spikes (New Year’s, summer fitness) could boost engagement.
	•  If users are engaging free tier deeply (e.g., maxing 3 sessions/week), upgrades may lag but come later—track day 7/30 retention.
Recommendations to Boost Sign-Ups/Upgrades
1.  Optimize onboarding for quick wins:
	•  Shorten AI interview or add skip/demo mode.
	•  Show teaser of “what full coaching looks like” early.
	•  Highlight upgrade benefits immediately after free plan generation (e.g., “Unlock unlimited habits + AI chat for $6/mo”).
2.  Test free tier tweaks:
	•  Temporarily allow 2–3 habits free or 7-day full Pro trial to hook users.
	•  Add in-app prompts after 2–3 sessions: “Loving your plan? Go unlimited!”
3.  Improve App Store page & ads:
	•  Ensure screenshots/videos demo AI personalization + results (testimonials: “30-day streaks!”).
	•  Target keywords like “AI habit coach,” “personalized habit builder” to attract users wanting more than basic tracking.
4.  Add conversion levers:
	•  Limited-time discount (e.g., first month $3 Pro).
	•  Lifetime option (~$99–$149) for anti-subscription users.
	•  Email/SMS follow-up for installers (if you capture data).
5.  Track deeper metrics:
	•  Use App Store Connect + analytics (Firebase, RevenueCat if integrated) for session depth, free tier usage, upgrade screen views → taps.
	•  Cohort analysis: What % return day 2/7? Where do they drop?
6.  Scale cautiously: Run ads longer (aim 100–500 installs) before judging. A/B test creatives/keywords.
